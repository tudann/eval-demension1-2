A recycling yard sits in near darkness. A yellow dump truck is backed onto a bare concrete pad, its outline barely visible in the dark.
The truck bed begins to tilt up. The first pieces of broken glass slide off the back edge of the bed, catching faint glints of light.
The first bank of overhead lights snaps on. One hard pool of light lands on the pad.
The pour grows into a full avalanche of broken glass. The hard, see-through pieces tumble down, bounce off the concrete, and pile up into a heap.
The second bank of lights switches on. Half the yard jumps one clear step brighter, and glints multiply across the falling glass.
The camera pushes in slowly and low from the side of the pad, keeping the raised truck bed and the growing heap together in frame.
The third bank of lights comes on. Now the whole scene is bright and evenly lit, and every glass edge glitters.
The truck bed reaches full tilt, and the last of the glass slides down the steel.
The heap settles. The pieces still shift and sparkle under the flat bright light of all three banks.
Photorealistic night exterior, faceless satisfying industry reel, no people anywhere near the pour, no on-screen text.
